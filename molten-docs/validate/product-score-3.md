## Red flags

- 🟡 **A free incumbent covers a meaningful slice of the workflow** — `terraform plan`, which many teams already run (often in CI), is itself a manual drift-detection mechanism. The pitch needs to be explicit about what this tool adds beyond that — e.g. continuous/scheduled checks outside the normal apply cycle, alerting on drift the moment it happens, or auto-drafting the reconciliation PR.

One flag — well below the "≥3 → stop and reassess" threshold. The Pivot verdict here is driven by the score (29/50), not by red flags.

## Pivot options

1. **Narrow the job, not just the market.** Instead of general "drift detection" (which overlaps with what `terraform plan` already does), focus specifically on *incident-driven drift reconciliation*: detect a manual AWS console change as it happens (e.g. via CloudTrail/EventBridge) and immediately flag it — or draft the Terraform diff/PR needed to reconcile it. This targets the exact 2am scenario from Round 1 rather than periodic scanning a free tool partially covers.
2. **Change the buyer, not the product.** Instead of selling to individual small teams one at a time, target MSPs/consultancies that manage AWS infrastructure on Terraform for multiple clients. One sale covers many environments — this both improves the unit-economics picture (criterion 6) and gives a sharper distribution path (a smaller number of larger accounts, reachable via MSP communities).
3. **Anchor "why now" to an external shift, not your own tooling.** Replace "AI makes it easier to build" with a real demand-side trigger — e.g. a recent change in AWS or Terraform's own drift-related APIs/features that makes near-real-time detection newly practical. This would strengthen both the timing score and the "AWS-native, lightweight" differentiation by giving it a concrete technical hook.
